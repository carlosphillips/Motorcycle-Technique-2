## Recommended Motorcycle Schools

The following list includes street, track, and a few dirt-oriented schools that I have done personally and highly recommend. For those interested in track days or other advanced riding schools, some great online resources are www.trackdaymag.com and www.motorcycle-usa.com, which publish updated schools and track days on their calendars. Roadracing World & Motorcycle Technology (www.roadracingworld.com) also has a comprehensive annual Track Day Directory with lots of great information and listings of current schools and events.

Total Control

Of course, the best way to put the skills in this book into practice is to take a Total Control Advanced Riding Clinic where you will have professional instructors coach you through each of the techniques. They are available nationwide and internationally to any rider with 3,000 or more street miles. Students are guaranteed to have a specific, measureable improvement in their riding. There are also beginner, intermediate, track, and off-road versions of the training available at select locations.

(800) 943-5638, www.totalcontroltraining.net

American Supercamp

Danny Walker’s dirt-based school uses Yamaha TTR125s in a safe, comfortable atmosphere with plenty of seat time and video feedback of your riding. While the cornering techniques are designed for the dirt and shouldn’t be used on the road, the skills you will learn for throttle control, balance, and traction management will help any experienced street or off-road rider improve. It’s also a lot of fun.

(970) 674-9434, www.americansupercamp.com

California Superbike School

The California Superbike School (CSS) was started in 1980 by motorcycle trainer and author Keith Code. CSS has expanded internationally into 18 countries and its graduates have gone on to win 53 World and National championships. The school’s curriculum continues to evolve with Keith’s research into what he calls the “art of cornering.”

(323) 224-2734, www.superbikeschool.com

MotoMark1

MotoMark1 offers police-style training in day- and week-long versions. It teaches precision maneuvering classes based on the skills taught in his book Maximum Control. Radio communications provide instant feedback while riding and video is utilized in the classroom to dissect problems and point out successes. Unlike many police-style courses, their instructors are friendly, encouraging, and treat you with respect.

(919) 637-0947, www.motomark1.com

MotoVentures

The MotoVentures Dirt First approach to motorcycle rider training is to utilize lightweight dirt bikes to teach a wide variety of people how to ride motorcycles safely and proficiently. Dirt First’s curriculum is detailed in Gary LaPlante’s book; How to Ride Off-Road Motorcycles. Dirt First training is designed for beginners to experts and includes dirt bike, off-road, motocross, trials, dual-sport, adventure bike, and street bike riders.

(877) 260-6686, www.motoventures.com

Stayin’ Safe Motorcycle Tours

Stayin’ Safe pioneered on-street rider coaching in the United States in 1993 and was developed to introduce advanced “road reading” techniques in real-world environments. Refined over more than two decades, the Stayin’ Safe method combines highly enjoyable motorcycle tours and proven street strategies with real-time coaching techniques. Bike-to-bike communications allow real-time feedback.

(724) 771-2269, www.stayinsafe.com

Track Day Provider Schools

Many track day providers also offer riding schools like Team Pro-Motion’s Advanced Rider Training. www.teampromotion.com. Here Glen Goldman explains the layout of New Jersey Motorsports Park to students.

Cone Home

If you’d like to practice the techniques of Total Control—or any other training protocol—in a parking lot, some cool 5x5x2-inch cones are available from www.totalcontroltraining.net to set up the exercises.
